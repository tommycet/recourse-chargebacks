02 — Evidence bundle as a standard

Every transaction emits a signed, hash-committed record.

Not another escrow contract — those are hackathon-saturated. The unbuilt thing is the institution around the escrow, transplanted from 50 years of card-network dispute architecture into machine speed. A standard evidence bundle closes the x402 receipts hole (issue #1645 — "in-memory and lost on server restart") while keeping bundles small (<1KB, hash-committed, not raw payload) for verifiable replay.

{ "rulebookVersion": "v1.0", "requestDigest": "sha3(keccak256('buy-summary-agent'))", "responseDigest": "sha3(output-hash)", "contentDigest": "sha3(delivered-content)", "txHash": "0xdead...", "timestamp": 1753574400, "signer": "0xAA70...", "hash()": "keccak256(canonicalJSON(this))", "toJSON()": "serialisable file for audit / EU MiCA / VAT export" }

Canonical JSON ordering (sorted keys, no whitespace) → deterministic hash → sybil-resistant reputation signal.

03 — Tiered arbiter, not a judge

Tier 0: instant. Tier 1: machine-speed. Tier 2: human appeal.

Card networks' genius was liability rules, not perfect judgment. We replicate that: Tier-0 verifies tx hash + contentDigest match + automated test suite passes → instant settlement. Tier-1 applies the published rulebook (TEE-hosted, no hidden weights) to the evidence bundle — machine-speed representment. Tier-2 is a human appeal, only for disputes above $500. The loser pays a 0.25% fee — aligning incentives and funding the layer.

Evidence bundles

Signed, hash-committed records per transaction. Closes the x402 #1645 receipts hole (in-memory → persistent) and feeds an exportable audit trail for EU MiCA / VAT compliance.

Tiered arbiter

Tier-0: objective verification (tx hash, contentDigest, latency SLA) settles instantly. Tier-1: AI arbiter in a TEE applies the public rulebook. Tier-2: human appeal.

Reputation flywheel

Payment volume can be gamed (86% of x402 Solana activity was "gamed"). A resolved dispute with real USDC moved is sybil-resistant by construction — the dataset moat.

05 — Honest risks

Three risks — with real mitigations.

Risk 1 — x402 Foundation standardizes disputes first. The Foundation (Google, Visa, AWS, Circle, Anthropic, Cloudflare) could add a dispute layer. Mitigation: become the reference implementation inside that standard process — standards bodies adopt working code, not specs. Our PR to #1645 is already that reference.

Risk 2 — organic agent commerce stays small. The 86% gaming figure is real; volume may not grow. Mitigation: the evidence/receipts layer has standalone demand today — enterprise fleets need "close the books" for agent spend (Sequoia: "the next legendary company will just close the books"). The dispute layer becomes the reputation trust layer either way.

Risk 3 — adversarial evidence gaming the AI arbiter. Launch objective-tiers-only (Tier-0 covers 80%+ of disputes by value), expand to semi-objective (schema conformance, latency SLAs) as the public rulebook hardens. TEE-hosted arbiter + signed attestations make gaming cost exceed dispute value.
