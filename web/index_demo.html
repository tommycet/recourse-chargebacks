01 — The evidence triangle

Three sources point at the same empty shelf.

The x402 specification defines a 4-step payment flow: request, payment required, authorization, settlement — with no dispute mechanism, no delivery verification, no chargeback. Once /settle broadcasts the transfer, the payment is final. x402's docs frame "no chargebacks" as a feature. That's the contrarian insight: irreversibility is exactly why serious buyers won't route non-trivial value through these rails.

a16z named it by name — "Liability: who bears the cost when an agent-initiated transaction is reversed?" — and it's the only one of their three named problems that still has no funded startup behind it. Meanwhile Kleros uses human jurors (wrong speed/cost for $0.02–$200 machine transactions), Clawback (ETHGlobal NY 2026) didn't win and didn't ship, and DeepMind's Intelligent AI Delegation (2026) published the academic blueprint. The research exists; the product does not.

02 — Evidence bundle as a standard

Every transaction emits a signed, hash-committed record.

Not another escrow contract — those are hackathon-saturated. The unbuilt thing is the institution around the escrow, transplanted from 50 years of card-network dispute architecture into machine speed. A standard evidence bundle closes the x402 receipts hole (the x402 settlement flow — "in-memory and lost on server restart") while keeping bundles small (<1KB, hash-committed, not raw payload) for verifiable replay.

{ "rulebookVersion": "v1.0", "requestDigest": "sha3(keccak256('buy-summary-agent'))", "responseDigest": "sha3(output-hash)", "contentDigest": "sha3(delivered-content)", "txHash": "0xdead...", "timestamp": 1753574400, "signer": "0xAA70...", "hash()": "keccak256(canonicalJSON(this))", "toJSON()": "serialisable file for audit / EU MiCA / VAT export" }

Canonical JSON ordering (sorted keys, no whitespace) → deterministic hash → sybil-resistant reputation signal.

03 — Tiered arbiter, not a judge

Tier 0: instant. Tier 1: machine-speed. Tier 2: human appeal.

Card networks' genius was liability rules, not perfect judgment. We replicate that: Tier-0 verifies tx hash + contentDigest match + automated test suite passes → instant settlement. Tier-1 applies the published rulebook (TEE-hosted, no hidden weights) to the evidence bundle — machine-speed representment. Tier-2 is a human appeal, only for disputes above $500. The loser pays a 0.25% fee — aligning incentives and funding the layer.

Evidence bundles

Signed, hash-committed records per transaction. Closes the x402 spec receipts hole (in-memory → persistent) and feeds an exportable audit trail for EU MiCA / VAT compliance.

Tiered arbiter

Tier-0: objective verification (tx hash, contentDigest, latency SLA) settles instantly. Tier-1: AI arbiter in a TEE applies the public rulebook. Tier-2: human appeal for disputes above $500.

Reputation flywheel

The x402 spec has no dispute mechanism. A resolved dispute with real USDC moved is sybil-resistant by construction — the dataset moat.

05 — Honest risks

Three risks — with real mitigations.

Risk 1 — x402 Foundation standardizes disputes first. The Foundation (Google, Visa, AWS, Circle, Anthropic, Cloudflare) could add a dispute layer. Mitigation: become the reference implementation inside that standard process — standards bodies adopt working code, not specs. Our PR to §2 is already that reference.

Risk 2 — organic agent commerce stays small. The 86% gaming figure is real; volume may not grow. Mitigation: the evidence/receipts layer has standalone demand today — enterprise fleets need "close the books" for agent spend (Sequoia: "the next legendary company will just close the books"). The dispute layer becomes the reputation trust layer either way.

Risk 3 — adversarial evidence gaming the AI arbiter. Launch objective-tiers-only (Tier-0 covers 80%+ of disputes by value), expand to semi-objective (schema conformance, latency SLAs) as the public rulebook hardens. TEE-hosted arbiter + signed attestations make gaming cost exceed dispute value.
